AI+ ORGANIZATION FRAMEWORK – DETAILED STEPS

A deep-dive into each framework step: what it is, how it works and what success looks like.

Step 1 of 6
1
VALUE (WHY)
Defines the core outcomes the organization wants to achieve with AI.
ITIL SVS PMBOK
Why It's Needed
Without a clear definition of value, AI efforts become technology-driven and lose focus. Value alignment ensures every AI initiative is tied to business priorities and creates measurable impact.
How It Helps
  • Aligns AI investments with business strategy
  • Prioritizes what matters most
  • Provides a clear North Star for decision-making
  • Enables consistent success measurement
VALUE DIMENSIONS
Efficiency
Do things faster and at lower cost. Reduce time, manual effort and operational spend.
Quality
Do things better with fewer errors. Improve accuracy, consistency and reliability.
Capability
Enable new outcomes and possibilities. Create new products, services and business models.
Framework Alignment: ITIL SVS + PMBOK

ITIL Service Value System (SVS) frames value as co-creation between the organization and its stakeholders—not purely internal benefit. Each AI initiative should ask: what value does this create for end-users and partners, not just the business?

PMBOK Benefit Realization Plan:

  • Map each AI initiative to an explicit benefits owner
  • Define the baseline state (current), target state (expected), and realization timeline
  • Schedule a Benefits Realization Review at 30/90/180 days post-deployment
When value is clear, AI becomes a force multiplier for business outcomes—not just another tool.
2
USE CASES (WHERE)
Standardized ways AI is applied across the organization.
PMBOK
Why It's Needed
AI can be applied in many ways. A standardized set of use case types creates a common language, helps spot opportunities, and ensures repeatability at scale.
How It Helps
  • Identifies high-impact opportunities
  • Encourages reuse of patterns and learnings
  • Speeds up ideation and prioritization
  • Supports scaling AI across functions
USE CASE TYPES
Automation
AI replaces repetitive, rule-based tasks end-to-end.
Augmentation
AI assists humans to be more productive and effective.
Generation
AI creates new content, code, designs, or ideas.
Prediction
AI forecasts outcomes, trends or behaviors.
Optimization
AI recommends the best actions or allocation of resources.
Framework Alignment: PMBOK Stakeholder Performance Domain

PMBOK's Stakeholder Performance Domain introduces the Power/Interest Grid as a tool for prioritizing which use cases to pursue based on stakeholder influence and engagement level.

Grid quadrants:

  • High Power / High Interest: Manage closely
  • High Power / Low Interest: Keep satisfied
  • Low Power / High Interest: Keep informed
  • Low Power / Low Interest: Monitor

Risk note: Without stakeholder mapping, use case portfolios fragment—high-interest but low-power users drive pilots that never get executive sponsorship to scale.

Action: Score each use case candidate against: Who sponsors it? Who uses it? Who blocks it? Prioritize use cases that satisfy high-power / high-interest sponsors.

Use cases turn AI potential into practical, repeatable solutions that drive real business results.
3
OPERATING MODEL (HOW)
Defines how humans and AI work together in real workflows.
ITIL 4 Dimensions
Why It's Needed
AI doesn't create value by itself—people do. A clear operating model defines the right balance of human judgment and AI capabilities for different types of work.
How It Helps
  • Clarifies roles and responsibilities
  • Ensures the right level of human involvement
  • Builds trust and adoption
  • Improves decision quality and accountability
OPERATING MODEL MODES
Assistive
(Human in Control)
AI provides recommendations or information. Humans make decisions and take action.
Co-Pilot
(AI + Human Collaboration)
AI and humans collaborate throughout the process. Decisions are shared.
Autonomous
(AI in Control, Human Oversees)
AI executes tasks or decisions independently. Humans oversee, intervene when needed.
Framework Alignment: ITIL 4 Dimensions of Service Management

ITIL's 4 Dimensions model ensures AI services are designed holistically—not just as technology solutions.

  • Organizations & People: Define an "AI Service Owner" role per service—accountable for outcomes, not just delivery. Aligns to ITIL's service ownership model.
  • Information & Technology: Maps directly to the existing Foundation pillar (Data, Tools, Integration).
  • Partners & Suppliers: Formalize relationships with AI cloud providers (OpenAI, Azure AI, AWS Bedrock) via vendor management—SLAs, data handling agreements, exit strategies.
  • Value Streams & Processes: Map the end-to-end flow from data ingestion through AI inference to business output for each use case. Identify handoff points and failure modes.

Note: Every operating model mode (Assistive, Co-Pilot, Autonomous) maps to a different ITIL value stream configuration.

The right operating model maximizes AI impact while keeping humans in the loop where it matters.
4
FOUNDATION (WHAT ENABLES IT)
The essential data, tools and technology that make AI possible.
ITIL Design & Transition
Why It's Needed
AI is only as good as the foundation it runs on. Strong data, modern tools and seamless integration ensure AI can be trusted, scalable and impactful.
How It Helps
  • Provides high-quality, accessible data for AI
  • Ensures the right tools and models are available
  • Enables secure and seamless integration
  • Builds a scalable, future-ready AI platform
FOUNDATION PILLARS
Data
High quality, accessible and governed data (structured, unstructured and tacit knowledge).
Tools / Models
AI/ML models, LLMs, platforms and automation tools.
Integration
APIs, systems, workflows and infrastructure integration that enable AI to work at scale.
Framework Alignment: ITIL Service Design & Transition

Treat each Foundation component (Data pipelines, AI models, Integration APIs) as a service with a formal Service Design Package—not merely a technical artifact.

  • Internal AI Service Catalogue: Publish a catalogue of available AI capabilities (e.g., "Document Summarization Service", "Demand Forecasting API") with SLAs, data requirements, and approved use cases. This prevents redundant builds and enables reuse.
  • CI/CD Pipelines as Consumable Services: Model training pipelines, prompt versioning, and deployment pipelines should be standardized, documented, and available to all teams—not owned by individual project teams.
  • ITIL Service Transition: Before promoting any AI capability to production, run a formal transition gate: configuration baseline, test evidence, rollback plan, and service acceptance criteria.
A strong foundation reduces risk, increases performance and unlocks the full potential of AI.
5
GOVERNANCE (CONTROL & TRUST)
Ensures responsible, ethical and compliant use of AI across the organization.
NIST AI RMF COBIT PMBOK
Why It's Needed
AI introduces new risks—privacy, bias, security, and compliance. Governance provides guardrails that protect people, the business and the brand.
How It Helps
  • Protects data, privacy and security
  • Mitigates bias and inaccuracies
  • Ensures regulatory compliance
  • Builds transparency and trust
  • Enables responsible innovation
GOVERNANCE AREAS
Privacy & Security
Protect data, ensure access controls and maintain security.
Risk (Bias & Accuracy)
Identify and mitigate bias, inaccuracies and risks.
Compliance
Meet regulatory and legal obligations.
Auditability & Control
Ensure transparency, traceability and human control.
Model Risk vs Output Risk
Model risk: the model behaves unexpectedly (drift, bias, miscalibration). Output risk: the model's output causes harm in context (hallucination accepted as fact, incorrect recommendation acted upon). Both require distinct monitoring and response plans.
Framework Alignment: NIST AI RMF + COBIT + PMBOK

NIST AI RMF Govern-Map-Measure-Manage Cycle:

Govern
Map
Measure
Manage
  • Govern: Establish AI risk culture, policies, accountability structures (Ethics Review Board as formal IT Governance body per COBIT)
  • Map: Identify context, categorize AI use by risk tier (model risk vs output risk—these are distinct)
  • Measure: Quantify risks—bias metrics, hallucination rates, model drift indicators
  • Manage: Respond using PMBOK Risk Response Plans—separate plans for model drift risk vs output quality risk

COBIT Addition: Separate the "AI Ethics Owner" structurally from the Execution team. This role reports to the Governance function, not to delivery. Mirrors COBIT's separation of IT governance from IT management.

6
EXECUTION (HOW IT HAPPENS)
How the organization delivers AI transformation and realizes value.
PMBOK Tailoring ITIL Continual Improvement
Why It's Needed
Strategy without execution doesn't create value. A clear execution approach, the right roles, and disciplined delivery turn AI strategy into measurable outcomes at scale.
How It Helps
  • Provides a clear path from idea to impact
  • Ensures the right people and skills are in place
  • Focuses effort on high-impact opportunities
  • Drives adoption and change management
  • Delivers measurable outcomes continuously
EXECUTION COMPONENTS
Approach
Choose the right delivery approach: framework-driven for scale and consistency, hierarchical (top-down) for alignment and control, or pragmatic (bottom-up) for speed and innovation.
Roles
Define and align key roles: Explorer (discover), Operator (execute), Architect (design), Orchestrator (scale and govern).
Prioritization
Focus on high-impact use cases based on business impact, feasibility and effort.
Adoption
Build capability, drive training and manage change for lasting adoption.
Framework Alignment: PMBOK Tailoring + ITIL Continual Improvement

PMBOK Tailoring Principle: Delivery approach is not one-size-fits-all.

  • Predictive (Waterfall) lifecycle → use for Foundation building (data platforms, MLOps pipelines, governance frameworks). Requirements are known; scope is bounded.
  • Adaptive (Agile) lifecycle → use for Generative AI pilots and use-case experimentation. Requirements emerge; outcomes are uncertain.
  • Hybrid → use for scaling proven pilots into enterprise-wide solutions.

ITIL 7-Step Improvement Process for the Measure Impact loop:

  1. Identify the improvement strategy
  2. Define what you will measure
  3. Gather the data
  4. Process the data
  5. Analyze the data
  6. Present and use the information
  7. Implement improvement

Note: The "7-step process" maps directly to this playbook's Measure Impact stage in the Workflow section.

Effective execution ensures AI initiatives are delivered on time, on budget and at the quality needed to create lasting business value.
